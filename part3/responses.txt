2. Pick one data source to add to this pipeline: player tracking data (29 skeletal points per player at 60 frames per second) or a text corpus of scouting reports and internal notes. Name one question a coach or executive could ask that becomes answerable only with that source. Describe how you would represent, retrieve, and validate it, and where the current embed.py / rag.py design would break.

I would add a text corpus of scouting reports and internal notes. A coach could ask: "Which prospects do our scouts consider best at recognizing defensive traps and find open teammate, and where do the evaluations disagree?" The existing game results, box scores, recaps, and injury notes do not contain those scouting judgments.

I would store each report with its author, date, version, player identifiers, and access permissions. I would split reports into sections or paragraphs, preserving document IDs and page references, then embed each chunk.

Retrieval would combine keyword and vector search with player, date, and permission filters. I would retrieve neighboring paragraphs for context and preserve differing assessments with their authors and dates.

Validation would require citations supporting each claimed evaluation. I would build a manually reviewed set of questions covering agreement, conflicting reports, and missing evidence, measuring retrieval coverage and whether answers accurately represent the sources.

The current embed.py treats existing source rows as document units. So long reports would need to be chunked and version tracked. In rag.py, _expand_game_ids() and subsequent joins organize evidence around games. Prospect reports may describe several games or no specific game, so retrieval and citation handling would need to support document-based evidence independently of game IDs.
